Personnel File — the whole staff

One person. One robot. One jar.

OOZEMeter is run by one person — the Operator — who wanted a single honest answer to a question the news never answers plainly: how bad is it, actually? Every outlet has a take. Nobody has a number you can check.

So this facility computes one. The Ooze Level is a 0–100 score of U.S. economic stress, built from public data anyone can pull: gas prices from the EIA, mortgage rates from Freddie Mac, unemployment from the BLS, card and auto delinquencies from the New York Fed, inflation from the CPI. The recipe is published, the scale is pegged to history — the calmest month since 2003 reads 10, the worst of the financial crisis reads 90 — and the raw output sits one click away, always.

The staff

The Operator decides what gets built, reads every report before you do, and outranks everyone on exactly one question: is it still fun?

OOZEBOT is the facility's editorial engine — a deterministic script, not a chatbot. Every clause it writes is driven by a number in the data, and an integrity gate blocks anything that isn't. It signs its own work "Generated by OOZEBOT."

The weekly edition is different and says so. Its prose is drafted by a language model working from an evidence packet it cannot edit and a score it cannot compute, then checked figure by figure and approved by a named human before anything is sent. It signs "Drafted with AI · approved by" that person. A byline that named the wrong engine would be a falsehood rather than a style choice, so the three categories — generated, model-drafted, and operator dispatch — are spelled out in the editorial policy.

A collection robot fetches fresh government data daily and refuses to publish anything implausible. When a feed goes stale, the site says so on the reading itself instead of quietly serving yesterday as today.

The rules this site refuses to break

No fake numbers, ever. If data isn't connected, the jar reads zero and says OFFLINE. Simulated figures (the state rankings, for now) keep their labels until real feeds ship.

No forecasts. The facility measures; it does not prophesy. You will never read a prediction here dressed up as a reading.

No black boxes. Weights, formula, methodology changes — all published, with a version history. When the formula changes, the change is logged, not hidden.

OOZEMeter is an educational instrument, not financial advice. It exists to make one confusing thing legible — and to be a small joy to check, the way a weather app is. If the jar ever stops being fun, the Operator has failed at the only job the board can't veto.

Say hello

The facility lives in the open at github.com/ArringtonC/oozemeter — the code, the data, and every sealed reading. Found a number that looks wrong? Open an issue. Corrections are logged publicly per the correction policy.
