Phase 2 mockup — ANSWER FIRST

OOZEMeter homepage · July 2026 payload · one of four phase-2 directions

Why this direction

The panel scored integrity 8.8 and first-time comprehension 3.5. That gap is not an authoring problem — the paragraph that explains the month already exists, already passes the narrative gate, and renders nowhere. So this direction does the one thing that closes the gap directly: it makes EDITORIAL.story the hero of the page and demotes the figure to a supporting plate. A stranger reads the finding before the number, in the generator's own words, at reading size.

The stated risk of this direction is that it stops feeling like an instrument and starts feeling like a blog. Three things beat that, and they are the actual design work here: (1) the lede is set in the instrument's own monospace face, not a reading face — it reads as a printout, not a column; (2) every figure inside the lede is tinted, underlined, and repeated in a permanently-visible source strip six lines below it, so no claim is ever more than one glance from its citation; (3) immediately under the lede sits the arithmetic — a ledger of what the reading is made of, ranked by contribution, whose column foots to the printed whole. A blog does not show its work below the fold. This one shows it above.

What I deliberately did NOT do

  • No second composite, no confidence interval, no per-line sparkline, no personalization, no second cross-check per line. The audit killed all five. INDICATORS[].spark stays deleted rather than implemented.
  • I did not write a single new sentence of data prose. Every claim on the product surface is verbatim from data/editorial.json, the Editorial Constitution, or copy already drafted and Constitution-checked in research/forensic/07-ux.md §4.3 and mockups/phase2/02-comprehension.md §4.2. Appendix B lists which string came from where. Phase 2's own finding is that this is a rendering gap; inventing better copy would be solving the wrong problem, and hand-written numbers are what §10 forbids.
  • I did not render the household paragraph ("what a household would notice"), even though it is Reader Promise §2 item 3 and it is generated today. Its live July text contains "steady employment kept paychecks coming" — the exact false positive the cross-check module exists to mark. Publishing it needs the generator to emit a markable clause; publishing it unmarked would put a known falsehood at reading size. Named as a slot in the backlog, not faked here.
  • I did not sort anything by Math.abs(delta). Biggest movers get one small, separately-labelled strip under the ledger. They are never the sort key of the main list (encoding law L2).
  • I did not put a named next-release date on the page. The Constitution asks for one; the payload does not carry one and I will not invent a date. The slot renders updatedLabel verbatim and the gap is logged.
  • No boot sequence, no count-up, no reveal stagger, no motion of any kind. The boot costs 2,220 ms of a 30-second budget establishing genre, and startCountUp() is requestAnimationFrame-driven so prefers-reduced-motion cannot stop it. Answer-first means the answer is on screen at first paint.
  • No green tick on the quiet state, no siren on the loud one. A green tick is a lie of scope and overloads --green, which already means "stress fell" on this site.
  • --ward appears in :root and is used by nothing. No household surface on this page carries it — not the dissent mark, not an evidence link, not a state chip.
  • No hover-only information anywhere. Every gloss, unit and source is visible without interaction. The one <details> on the page contains Level-5 evidence only, and nothing above it depends on opening it (L8).

Fonts

CSP blocks CDNs, so no webfont loads here. The --mono and --display token strings are byte-identical to lab.css; with IBM Plex Mono and Unbounded absent they fall through to the system monospace and system sans stacks I appended. In production the lede renders in IBM Plex Mono, which is the face this layout is designed for.

What doesn't add up?

▲ Meaningful contradiction detected

Two job numbers point opposite ways this month.

The unemployment rate fell to 4.1%. Over the same month the share of American adults with a job fell to 58.9%, and the share either working or looking fell to 61.4%.

Both can be true at once. The unemployment rate only counts people who are looking for work. In July, 264,000 people stopped looking, so they left the count. Fewer people looking makes the rate go down even when fewer people are working.

The jar reads the unemployment rate. It does not read the number of people working. So this month's employment line — 2 of the jar's 26 ounces — is measuring the friendlier of the two facts.

Appendix B — provenance of every string on the product surface

  • The lede — data/editorial.json → story, verbatim, with its two {{s:2026-07}} tokens resolved. Build hazard, flagged: per 00-references.md A1, narrative-check.js:203 now scans index.html and fails the build on unresolved tokens. Stamping story into the static file trips the gate; injecting it client-side without resolveClaims() ships {{s:2026-07}} to readers on a green build. The correct implementation is a client-side render through resolveClaims() (lab.js:235–249). Neither shortcut is acceptable and this mockup assumes the resolver.
  • Verdict line — editorial.json → verdict, verbatim. Unmoved and unstyled from production; it is the site's strongest comprehension asset and this direction does not touch it, only relocates it into the plate it glosses.
  • Band gloss — Editorial Constitution §3, verbatim: "That is Sticky territory — the band where normal economies live." The Constitution mandates it; the homepage omits it today.
  • Confidence footer — editorial.json → confidence, verbatim.
  • Ledger cells — data/latest.json fields only: value, contrib, delta. No prose.
  • Loud module copy — research/forensic/07-ux.md §4.3, verbatim (Constitution-checked clause by clause in that document). Authored form: {{x:UNRATE:value}}, {{x:EMRATIO:value}}, {{x:CIVPART:value}}, {{x:CLF16OV:delta_abs}}, {{line:jobs:contrib}}, {{s:2026-07}}. Those forms need a resolver extension that does not exist yet.
  • Quiet module copy — 02-comprehension.md §4.2, using its first-edition variant. I used that variant specifically to avoid printing a streak number I cannot verify. No {{cc:streak}} value was invented.
  • Level-5 evidence block — 07-ux.md §4.5, verbatim including its LIMITS section. The LIMITS block is not decoration; it is the thing that stops this module becoming a doom feed.
  • Everything else (column heads, the "what the 26 is made of" heading, the lead-in sentence, the AUX note, the movers label) is interface copy written by me and contains no datum that can go stale. Where a count appears it describes components, not the economy.

Appendix C — what this direction fixes, in one list

  • CRITICAL 1 fixed. EDITORIAL.story is the hero. It is the first content a reader meets and it is set at reading size.
  • CRITICAL 2 fixed. Nothing sorts by Math.abs(delta). Housing 7, credit 6, auto 5, gas 4 lead the ledger — 22 of 26 ounces at full typographic rank, against the printed whole, with the column footing to 26.
  • The zero-is-up bug. delta === 0 renders "— unchanged" in --muted, not "▲0" in --amber. That is 18 of 26 ounces currently mis-stated on the live homepage.
  • The unit collision. Every signed number ships with a unit — "down 3 points", "7 oz of 26" — and the observed value is never adjacent to a bare stress delta.
  • Constitution §4 on card faces. Every named line carries its observed value in the same row. Level: 58 appears nowhere; stress points are internal and are not printed to the reader at all on this page.
  • Three different zeros, disambiguated. Financial conditions is weighted and rounds to 0 oz; the two auxiliary sensors are zero-weight by design and sit outside the total. Today all three render identically.
  • The scope trap. data-level is on the page root, so one number renders in one green.
  • Accessibility floor. No type below .72rem and no body text below .75rem; --dim used only on --bg-tinted ground, never as a datum; every interactive target ≥44px (nav 44, band chip 46, summary 44, dissent link 44); real <table> with scope and a caption; direction carried by glyph and word as well as hue; tables stack rather than shrink below 720px; zero motion.
  • Reader Promise §2 items 1–2 delivered. Item 3 (household) is withheld with cause. Item 4 (a named date) is still not delivered — the payload has no next-release date and I did not invent one. That is an open gap, not a solved one.

Appendix D — the honest weaknesses of this direction

  • The lede is grade 9.0 / Fog 13.2. The verdict line it displaces from the top is grade 4.5. This direction promotes the site's hardest sentence to the largest type. It is the sentence that carries the causes, so I stand by it — but the 30-second test should be re-run empirically before this ships, and if strangers stall on it the fix is a generator change to story, not a layout change.
  • A 396-character monospace paragraph is a lot of first impression. On a 360px phone the lede is roughly 11 lines before the reader reaches the plate. The mitigation here is that the plate is sticky on desktop and immediately follows on mobile, but a returning visitor who only wants the number now scrolls past a paragraph they have read.
  • The empty-state roster is counterfactual for July 2026 and is labelled as such on the divider. A reviewer is entitled to say that a quiet state which has never actually published is an unproven design.
