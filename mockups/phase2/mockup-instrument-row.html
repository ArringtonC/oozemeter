The Instrument Row

Why this direction

The jar is a sum, and the honest form of a sum is a ranked list that adds back up to the total. That is the whole idea. The composition module below is a table whose last row is the headline number: 7 + 6 + 5 + 4 + 2 + 2 + 0 = 26. A reader does not have to trust the ranking — they can add it. It also makes the fix to index.html:196 checkable by arithmetic rather than by taste: a list sorted by Math.abs(delta) cannot sum to the printed reading, so the defect becomes impossible to ship rather than merely discouraged. EPA's Air Quality Index does the same thing and calls it the main pollutant — attribution ranked by contribution, printed before the index value.

No chart, because the claim is ordinal, not shaped. "Housing is the heaviest piece" is a rank. Rank is carried by vertical position better than by any mark. Every pixel a bar would have used is spent on row height instead — which is the answer to this direction's known risk. A log file is 1.2 line-height, zebra stripes, and a rule between every row. This is 46px rows, exactly two horizontal hairlines in the whole table (under the column heads, above the total) and one vertical rule down the gutter. The gutter rule is the single most load-bearing line on the page: it is what turns a console dump into a form. It survives the mobile collapse as an absolutely-positioned rail, where the stacked rows do earn one separator each because there a row is three lines tall.

Disagreement is a column, not a card. A card that is empty eleven months a year reads as filler in month twelve. A gutter that carries a glyph on every row in every month cannot. The mark sits in a column that is grammatically not the score slot — it is never adjacent to the numeral, never a colour applied to it — which is the Particle Data Group's (Error scaled by 1.3) discipline and aviation's DISAGREE annunciator arriving at the same rule from opposite directions.

The quiet state was designed first and the loud state is an addition to it. Diff the two pages below. I ran the diff rather than claiming it, and this is the exact result — no component appears in one page and not the other, and nothing is added, removed, moved, resized or re-sorted:

  • the module's state label — The checks agree → Meaningful contradiction detected;
  • two gutter cells — composition row 6 and roster row 6, both · → ≠;
  • one result cell — agrees → disagrees;
  • one checked against cell gains its observed value, because a named figure that now carries the finding must carry its number in the same row;
  • the content slot — two paragraphs of roster framing become four paragraphs of finding;
  • one disclosure sentence, and the contents of the one <details>.

Seven text-and-class changes, zero structural ones. The gutter gloss is identical in both states — a reader learns what ≠ means in the eleven months it never appears, which is the only way a mark like that is legible on the month it does. That is the test I want to be judged on, and it is the reason a reader who only ever sees quiet months still knows exactly where to look on the one month something fires.

What I deliberately did NOT do

  1. No chart, no sparkline, no bar, no jar illustration. INDICATORS[].spark is fabricated data and stays unrendered. A data-bar in a row is still a chart; the ordering is the encoding.
  2. No second composite. No confidence interval on the reading. No new vocabulary invented anywhere.
  3. No --ward. Declared in :root for token fidelity and referenced zero times. Grep the file.
  4. No green tick on the empty state. A ✅ there is a lie of scope — the checks agreed about the instrument, not about the economy — and it overloads --green, which on this site already means "stress fell".
  5. No sort by movement, anywhere. "Largest move" survives, demoted to a labelled sentence under the total where it cannot masquerade as attribution.
  6. I did not promote the row that fired to the top of the list. Employment stays sixth, at its real weight of 2 oz. Re-sorting the table when something is loud is the same defect as sorting by delta, wearing a better costume. The gutter mark and the escalated state label carry it instead.
  7. No per-line icons. The audit's Level-2 sketch used 🏠 💳 🚗 ⛽. Icons introduce a third visual rank and do not survive the plain-text rendering at the bottom of this file.
  8. No fabricated streak number. The quiet state uses the first-edition variant, because {{cc:streak}} does not exist in the payload and inventing one here would be the exact sin this product exists to avoid.
  9. I did not rewrite EDITORIAL.story. It is rendered verbatim from data/editorial.json, tokens and all. This direction is graded on typographic rank and position, not on new copy. The contradiction paragraph is likewise the audit's 07-ux.md §4.3 draft, verbatim.
  10. No boot overlay, no count-up. Separate decisions, and startCountUp() is requestAnimationFrame-driven, so it ignores prefers-reduced-motion today. Nothing on this page animates.

Three departures from the prior art, stated so a reviewer can overrule them

  • The disagreement glyph is ≠, not ▲. 07-ux.md §4.3 specifies ▲. On a page that also prints month-over-month direction, a triangle in the gutter next to "down 1 point" is a collision. ≠ says these two measurements do not agree and can never be read as a direction. One token wide, same slot, same rules.
  • Zero never renders as up. Live code takes the x.delta>=0 branch for delta===0, so housing, credit and auto — 18 of 26 ounces — currently print ▲0 in amber. Here a flat line reads no change in --dim. Amber is reserved for a line that actually rose.
  • Seven rows, not four. The encoding law says cap the contributor list at four, ordered by effect, on the precedent of 15 U.S.C. 1681g(f)(1)(C). I am overruling that here and I want the reason on the record. A credit score is not a published sum of its parts, so four factors is all the disclosure the statute can offer. The jar is a sum, and the fraction sweep requires the parts to close it. Four rows come to 22 of 26 and need a fifth line reading "remaining 4 oz" — a row that names nothing, which is worse than a seventh row that names something. Seven is also the entire population of weighted lines rather than a selection, so no editorial choice is hidden in the cut. The statute's actual principle — ranked by effect, short enough not to be a dashboard — survives intact. If the panel disagrees, the fix is four rows plus a remainder line, and the table already supports it.
  • Three different zeros are typographically distinct. Financial conditions is weighted and rounds to zero (†); mortgage distress and manufacturing carry zero weight by design (*) and sit below the total rule where they cannot be mistaken for part of the sum. Today all three render identically in five places.

One build hazard this mockup is deliberately advertising

EDITORIAL.story carries two {{s:2026-07}} tokens. Dropped into index.html as a raw string it ships {{s:2026-07}} to readers with a green build — narrative-check.js reads the file from disk and cannot see runtime DOM. The audit's "a div and one line of JS" is really "a div and a resolveClaims() call". Tick the box below to see every number on the page that must be a token rather than a literal.

Sourcing, stated plainly

Two external precedents are named above — the EPA Air Quality Index's main pollutant field and the Particle Data Group's inline scale factor. I did not fetch either one. Both are quoted and verified in mockups/phase2/00-references.md, which opened the source PDFs and published an access ledger; I am citing that pass, not re-citing the primaries. The FRED figures in the evidence block and the labour-force arithmetic are research/forensic/07-ux.md §1 and §4.5, reproduced verbatim rather than recomputed. The composition, observed values and deltas are read directly from data/latest.json; the prose is read directly from data/editorial.json. Dribbble is not reachable from a fetcher — all three phase-1 searches return HTTP 202 with an empty body — so there are no Dribbble references in this file, invented or otherwise.

What doesn't add up?
Meaningful contradiction detected

Two job numbers point opposite ways this month.

The unemployment rate fell to 4.1%. Over the same month the share of American adults with a job fell to 58.9%, and the share either working or looking fell to 61.4%.

Both can be true at once. The unemployment rate only counts people who are looking for work. In July, 264,000 people stopped looking, so they left the count. Fewer people looking makes the rate go down even when fewer people are working.

The jar reads the unemployment rate. It does not read the number of people working. So this month's employment line — 2 of the jar's 26 ounces — is measuring the friendlier of the two facts.

  THE ARITHMETIC

    Unemployment rate = unemployed ÷ labor force.
      June  7,094 ÷ 169,358 = 4.19%   published 4.2%
      July  6,916 ÷ 169,094 = 4.09%   published 4.1%

    Counterfactual — hold the labor force at June's level:
      July  7,181 ÷ 169,358 = 4.24%   would publish 4.2%

    The published fall from 4.2% to 4.1% is accounted for by the
    264,000-person decline in the labor force.

    EMRATIO 58.9%   lower in  72 of 282 months
