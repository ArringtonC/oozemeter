Phase 2 mockup · direction 5 of the gauntlet

THE LADDER — five rungs, one spine

Why this direction

The panel's two failing scores are explanation 4.5 and first-time comprehension 3.5, and both defects behind them are the same defect wearing two hats: the page publishes the score and withholds the reasoning. A homepage that answers "how bad" in 15 seconds and never answers "why" is a scoreboard, and a scoreboard cannot be audited. The ladder's bet is that the reasoning is not a page you click through to — it is the page, laid out so that stopping early is a designed outcome rather than a failure.

The direction's stated risk is that four stacked sections become a wall. Three devices beat that, and they are the whole design:

  • A spine, not five panels. One unbroken hairline runs the left edge through every rung, numbered 1–5. Five sections with a shared spine read as one object with five parts; five bordered cards read as five demands. The spine also tells a reader where the bottom is — the strongest anti-wall device there is, because a wall is only frightening when you cannot see its height.
  • Texture alternation is a hard rule. Air → grid → prose → panel → collapsed. No two adjacent rungs share a texture, so scrolling produces a rhythm rather than a smear. Rung 3 is the only prose on the page, which is why it reads as the explanation and not as more chrome.
  • A budget per rung. R1 four elements. R2 four rows plus one remainder line. R3 two paragraphs. R4 one paragraph plus a seven-row roster. R5 hidden until asked for. A rung that wants a fifth element has to take one from the rung above.

Everything below rung 2 is optional to the casual reader and mandatory to the sceptic. That is the only claim this mockup makes, and the ladder integrity test in 07-ux.md §3 is how to falsify it: give a stranger rungs 1–4 for July 2026, ask "how is the job market?", and the answer must be "unemployment looks better but fewer people are working, and the site says its own jar can't see that."

What is on this page

  • State A — the loud state. The full ladder with July 2026's real, verified payrolls-vs-unemployment contradiction firing on rung 4, and marked in place inside the rung-3 sentence it contradicts.
  • State B — the quiet state. The same ladder, same components, in a month where nothing fires. Designed first; State A is State B plus one paragraph and one mark.
  • The diff ledger at the bottom, component by component, so structural invariance is checkable rather than asserted.

What doesn't add up?

always visible
▲ Meaningful contradiction detected 1 of 7 checks disagrees

Two job numbers point opposite ways this month.

The unemployment rate fell to 4.1%. Over the same month the share of American adults with a job fell to 58.9%, and the share either working or looking fell to 61.4%.

Both can be true at once. The unemployment rate only counts people who are looking for work. In July, 264,000 people stopped looking, so they left the count. Fewer people looking makes the rate go down even when fewer people are working.

The jar reads the unemployment rate. It does not read the number of people working. So this month's employment line — 2 of the jar's 26 ounces — is measuring the friendlier of the two facts.

The quiet state is the bigger one, and it was designed first Seven monospace rows naming a real series and a real comparison are seven checkable facts. Restraint ledger

What I deliberately did not do

  • No second number, anywhere. No adjusted jar, no "true unemployment", no composite of the cross-checks. The reading is 26 in both states and rung 4 says so in writing.
  • No confidence interval, no error bar, no uncertainty band on the score. The disagreement is reported as a named state with a gutter mark, never as a modification of the numeral.
  • No sparklines. INDICATORS[].spark is fabricated and unrendered; the correct action is to delete it, not to give it a home in a mockup.
  • No second cross-check on any line. One per line, seven total, published in advance. Thirty checks is a dashboard and a dashboard is what this site exists not to be.
  • No chart. The ounce bar is 26 tick marks against a printed denominator, not a visualisation — it has no axis, no scale to misread and no interpolation. Rung 5 is text and tables.
  • No --ward. The token appears once, on the words "Ward M" in the footer firewall sentence, which is the only permitted use. No contradiction mark, evidence link, chip or tooltip borrows it.
  • No green tick and no red siren. No ✅, no ⚠, no alarm wash, no flicker. The loud state is louder by wording and position, because --red is the dimmest state token on this canvas and cannot be made to carry the escalation.
  • No animation of any kind. No count-up on the score, no reveal stagger, no jar motion. The live count-up is requestAnimationFrame-driven, so animation:none cannot stop it and a reader who asked for no motion still watches the headline number be wrong for 1600ms. A mockup should not reproduce a defect for atmosphere.
  • No boot sequence, no clearance check, no streak counter. Genre before subject on the one visit that decides trust; and a daily streak widget contradicts a monthly seal four pixels away.
  • No research rail, no share card, no ad slot, no personalisation. This mockup is the ladder column only. The rail is not wrong — it is simply not what is being tested here, and including it would let the ladder hide behind furniture.
  • No new editorial copy. Every sentence in rungs 3 and 4 is verbatim from the generator or from the audits. The explanation gap is a rendering gap, not an authoring gap, and a mockup that writes better prose is answering a question nobody asked.
  • No hand-typed score in prose. Token-derived numerals carry a dotted underline and name their token on hover. The three {{cc:*}} forms do not exist in the payload today and are labelled illustrative wherever they appear.

Build notes, if this direction is picked

  • EDITORIAL.story carries two {{s:2026-07}} tokens. Rendering it as a raw string ships {{s:2026-07}} to readers with a green build — the narrative gate reads index.html from disk and cannot see runtime DOM. It must go through resolveClaims(). This is the single most likely way to ship this rung broken.
  • The household paragraph has no field of its own; it is embedded in editorial.newsletter. Rung 3 needs it split out into editorial.household.
  • The correct contributor sort already exists at index.html:310, 114 lines below the defective one at :196, and today it renders only into the share card. Rung 2 is a move, not a build.
  • The x.delta>=0 branch puts zero on the up arrow in amber. Rung 2's held flat needs that branch split three ways before it is true.
  • {{cc:ran}}, {{cc:streak}} and {{cc:since}} need emitting from the payload and adding to resolveClaims(), which today handles five forms.
