Inspiration board:
making a contradiction obvious in five seconds

A checkpoint before any mockups exist. Veto anything here and the mockups never inherit it. Nothing in this document touches production code — this file lives in mockups/, is not linked from the site, and is not in the sitemap.

The problem, stated once

June → July 2026. Four numbers about the same labour market, pointing two different ways.

The headline
Unemployment 4.2% → 4.1%
Reads as improvement. Our score treated it as one.
BUT
Underneath
−23,000 jobs
Participation 61.5 → 61.4. Employment-population 59.0 → 58.9.
Why this is a design problem, not only a data problem The mechanism is one sentence — people who stop looking for work leave the unemployment calculation, so the rate improves while the labour market doesn't. But a reader has to already know that to see it. Every reference below is judged on one question: does it help someone who does not study economics notice the contradiction before they read a paragraph?
How these references were gathered — read this I browsed Dribbble directly in a real browser and looked at the designs, rather than reading text about them. Every reference marked SEEN is something I actually viewed and evaluated; the designer name and search are given so you can pull it up in seconds. References marked CONVENTION are established practice in a named field rather than a specific shot — no invented citations, no fabricated shot IDs. Where I could not verify something, it is not here.
A

Editorial — the claim outranks the data

Publication layout, where interpretation is the largest thing on the page and numbers are demoted to evidence beneath it.
Mansi Chauhan — “Most Risk Emerges Before Finance Can Respond”Seen
Dribbble · search: data storytelling
A muted olive/cream report screen whose headline is a finding, not a label. Charts sit below it, small, doing the work of proving the sentence. Nothing on the screen competes with the claim.
The move to borrowThe section header is the conclusion, written as a sentence. “Employment looks weaker than the headline suggests” — not “Employment.” Charts become footnotes to a claim.
The move, in Ooze's language
The headline improved.
The labour market didn't.

PAYROLLS−23,000
PARTICIPATION61.4% ↓
EMPLOYED SHARE58.9% ↓
Paperpillar — “TIME PASSED / THE PASSION DIDN'T”Seen
Dribbble · search: magazine editorial layout
An oversized hand-set headline in two clauses, the second reversing the first. The headline is the content; everything else is caption.
The move to borrowThe two-clause reversal is literally our story's grammar: “X improved. Y didn't.” Make that construction the standing headline form for every MIXED or DISTORTED signal. It is memorable, repeatable weekly, and needs no economics.
Tom Birch — dark editorial spreadsSeen
Dribbble · search: magazine editorial layout
Near-black pages, white multi-column type, a contents list set as a typographic index. No decoration at all. Confident because it is restrained.
The move to borrowProof that dark + typographic reads as publication rather than terminal. The difference is column measure and generous leading — dashboards pack, publications breathe. Ooze already has the palette; it needs the spacing.
ArtVersion — “Family Matters” report spreadSeen
Dribbble · search: annual report data editorial
Print spread where a handful of percentages are set in circles as callouts over imagery, with a bar chart alongside carrying the detail.
The move to borrowTwo tiers of number: three or four callout figures a reader is meant to remember, and everything else demoted to a chart they can ignore. Ooze currently gives all seven lines equal typographic weight.
B

Instrumentation — disagreement as a first-class state

Monitoring, aviation and medical displays, where “these two instruments disagree” is a designed condition rather than an edge case. On-brand for a containment lab.
Nikita Iarygin — alert builder with an expected bandSeen
Dribbble · search: anomaly detection
A line chart with a shaded expected range behind it. The series runs inside the band, then breaks out — and the breakout is the whole message. You read the anomaly without reading a number.
The move to borrowShow what we expected alongside what happened. Unemployment falling is only strange because payrolls normally rise with it. Draw the expectation and the violation in one object.
The move, in Ooze's language
EXPECTED IF THE LABOUR MARKET WERE IMPROVING
Payrolls left the expected band while the headline rate kept improving. The red segment is the contradiction.
AppSignal — “Triggered Alerts” listSeen
Dribbble · search: anomaly detection
A plain text list of alerts with word-states — Warning, Ongoing, Cooling down, Ended — and almost no colour or chartwork. It stays calm while reporting problems.
The move to borrowA word taxonomy instead of a colour taxonomy. Our three states (Confirmed / Mixed / Distorted) can be carried by typography and position alone, which is also the accessibility answer.

Instrument-disagree warnings (aviation)Convention
Established cockpit practice — e.g. IAS DISAGREE, ALT DISAGREE annunciations on transport aircraft, raised when redundant sensors diverge
When two sources of the same quantity disagree, the aircraft does not silently pick one and it does not average them. It raises a named condition telling the pilot the instruments disagree, because that fact is itself the safety-critical information.
The move to borrowThe single most transferable idea on this board. Our jar silently picked one source — the unemployment rate — and reported improvement. “DISAGREE” should be a publishable state of an indicator, displayed as prominently as a value.
Status-page severity languageConvention
Widely used across public status pages (operational / degraded / partial outage)
A graded vocabulary that reports trouble without alarm, and crucially has a state for working but not fully trustworthy — “degraded performance.”
The move to borrowOoze needs the equivalent of “degraded”: the number is published, and its reliability is qualified, in the same glance. That is exactly what a Distorted signal is.
C

Explanatory — reveal the hidden variable

Interfaces whose job is comprehension of a mechanism. The “ohhh” moment is the deliverable.
Avnish Poonia — anomalies highlighted inside the sheetSeen
Dribbble · search: anomaly detection
Rather than extracting anomalies into a separate panel, the offending cells are marked in place inside the spreadsheet, so you see the outlier and its context together.
The move to borrowDo not exile the contradiction to a sidebar. Mark it on the ledger where the indicator already lives, so the reader sees which line is suspect while looking at all of them.
Explorable explanations — the revealed termConvention
Established practice in explorable/interactive explanation (Bret Victor's “Explorable Explanations”; the genre that followed it)
A paradox is set up, then resolved by revealing a variable the reader could not see. The revelation is staged — expectation first, violation second, hidden term third.
The move to borrowThe three-beat structure for our employment story: (1) unemployment fell, (2) but jobs fell too, (3) because people stopped being counted. Beat three is the payoff and it should be a designed reveal, not a footnote.
The move, in Ooze's language — three beats
1 — Unemployment fell to 4.1%.
2 — But the economy lost 23,000 jobs.
3 — Because people who stop looking stop being counted.
Annotated-chart convention in data journalismConvention
Standard practice at major graphics desks — the reading of the chart is written onto the chart
Rather than a titled chart plus a caption elsewhere, the interpretation is placed directly on the plot at the moment it applies.
The move to borrowIf Ooze ever draws a line, the sentence goes on the line. No chart ships without its reading attached — which also enforces the house rule that interpretation must be labelled as interpretation.
D

The negative space — what we must not build

Gathered deliberately. This family exists to stop the other three producing something pretty and wrong.
The dark monitoring dashboard, as a genreSeen
Dribbble · search: monitoring dashboard dark — an entire page of them
Row upon row of equal-sized KPI tiles and small line charts. Every metric rendered at the same visual weight.
Why it fails our problemMetric equality. If everything is the same size, nothing tells you where to look — and our entire job is to say “look here, this one is lying.” A dashboard that treats all seven Ooze lines identically cannot express that.
What we are rejecting
4.1%UNEMP
3.5%CPI
$4.08GAS
6.69%MORT
Four facts, equal weight, no story. The reader learns nothing they could repeat.
Red/green as the semantic systemStructural finding
Observed across nearly every finance/analytics shot reviewed
Colour used to encode good vs bad, applied per-metric.
Why it fails our problemOur employment case is a green headline over red internals. A good/bad colour system collapses that into one valence and destroys the finding. We need to encode agreement vs disagreement — concordance, not virtue. That is a different axis and it needs a different visual channel: position, rule-weight, and the word itself.
Template “comparison” graphicsSeen
Dribbble · search: comparison chart annotation
Searching explicitly for annotated comparison returned almost entirely generic dashboards and slide templates — side-by-side columns that compare products, not signals that disagree about the same thing.
Why this matters more than it looksThis is the most useful negative result on the board: the design field has largely not solved “two measurements of one thing disagree.” There is no convention to copy. Whatever we build here is genuinely distinctive — and we should take the instrumentation answer (Family B) seriously precisely because engineering solved it and design didn't.

The harder case, held separately: oil

Employment is one signal lying. Oil is one signal telling two true stories at once — cheaper fuel genuinely helps a household budget, and falling oil can simultaneously warn of weakening demand. Nothing in any family above solves this cleanly, and a single card with one status word cannot hold it.

For your wallet
Genuinely good
Lower pump prices reduce household pressure. This is not a caveat — it is real relief.
AND
For the economy
Worth watching
If the fall is demand-driven rather than supply-driven, it is a warning, not a gift.
The design question to answer in the mockups Does a dual-reading indicator get one card that splits, two cards that cross-reference, or a different component type entirely? Whichever direction handles oil most gracefully is probably the right architecture, because oil is the harder of the two cases.

The biggest risk in this whole exploration That “What doesn't add up?” becomes a component and not a product identity. If it is a card, it will be empty most weeks and will read as filler — most months genuinely have no contradiction, and manufacturing one to fill the slot would be the worst possible outcome for a publication whose entire differentiator is not overstating what it knows. If it is the identity, then the honest empty state — “Nothing contradicts this week. The signals agree.” — is itself a valuable weekly message, and the product has a reason to exist between monthly seals. The mockups should test the empty state as seriously as the loud one.
