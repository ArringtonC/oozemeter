First, the thing that changes the whole problem

The investigation into the employment case turned up something I had wrong. Look at the four numbers again:

June → July 2026 · raw direction
↓Unemployment rate4.2 → 4.1
↓Nonfarm payrolls−23,000
↓Labour-force participation61.5 → 61.4
↓Employment-population ratio59.0 → 58.9
All four fell. On a raw direction axis they are four down-ticks in perfect agreement. To the eye, this data looks like consensus.
The contradiction is invisible until you polarise by meaning Every one of those four numbers went the same way. The disagreement only exists once you ask which direction is toward stress — and then unemployment separates from the other three, because falling unemployment means less stress while falling payrolls, participation and employment-share all mean more. The design job is not to show four numbers. It is to re-polarise them onto a shared stress axis so one line visibly leaves the pack. That single move converts a paragraph of economics into a shape you read in a second — and it is also the reason our own pipeline saw agreement where a human saw a problem.
The same four, re-polarised · toward stress ↑
One line goes the other way. No legend, no arrows, no colour required to carry it — the crossing is the message.
How these references were gathered — read this first Two independent methods. I browsed Dribbble in a real browser and looked at the designs (Seen), and I ran a separate research pass that fetched primary sources directly (Fetched). Established practice in a named field, rather than a single artefact, is marked Convention. No invented citations, no fabricated shot IDs; where a source could not be verified it was cut rather than passed through with a caveat. Two references were dropped in review for exactly that reason. Confidence notes appear where they matter.
A

Definitional — let the source indict itself

The strongest editorial position available: we are not second-guessing the data, we are showing you what the agency already publishes.
★ Our World in Data — the subtitle is the definitionFetched
ourworldindata.org — house pattern on every grapher page
The chart title is the bare indicator name; the subtitle is the agency's verbatim definition; the source is demoted below the plot; a standing “What you should know about this indicator” block sits underneath.
The move to borrow — possibly the best idea on this board Print the official definition and light up the four words that cause the whole problem. The mechanism is already inside the government's own wording. Nothing is asserted; the reader simply sees it.
The move, in Ooze's language
UNEMPLOYMENT RATE · AS THE BLS DEFINES IT
Share of the labour force without work, but actively looking for a job and available to start soon.
Stop looking, and you leave the measurement. The rate falls. Nobody got a job.
Confidence: highest in the set — page fetched and quoted directly.
★ BLS publishes the ladder that contradicts its own headlineFetched
The Employment Situation release · U-1 through U-6 · bls.gov/cps/definitions.htm
Six alternative measures of labour underutilisation are published every month. U-3 is the one the press quotes. U-4 adds discouraged workers, U-5 adds all marginally attached, U-6 adds involuntary part-time.
The move to borrowShow the headline as one rung, not a fact. This is the maximum-honesty move in the whole exploration: we are not disputing the number, we are publishing what the agency already publishes and the coverage ignores. “The headline is narrow, not wrong” becomes structural rather than argued.
The move, in Ooze's language
U-3the headline everyone quotes
U-4+ people who gave up looking
U-5+ everyone loosely attached to the workforce
U-6+ part-time because they can't get full-time
Sarah Leo — “Mistakes, we've drawn a few”, The Economist (2019)Verified via 3 sources
An Economist visual journalist auditing her own magazine's charts
She sorted the paper's own past chart failures into named categories — misleading, confusing, failing to make a point — and reprinted each flawed original beside her redesign at equal size.
The move to borrowShipped precedent for a serious publication printing the word “misleading” about its own work. That licenses our DISTORTED state. And the method: quote the original framing exactly, set your reframing beside it at equal weight, put one rule between them. Adjacency makes the argument; you never have to characterise anyone.
Confidence: existence and authorship solid across three independent write-ups; the original essay was not fetched, so treat the category names as secondary-sourced.
Mansi Chauhan — “Most Risk Emerges Before Finance Can Respond”Seen
Dribbble · search: data storytelling
A report screen whose headline is a finding rather than a label. Charts sit below it, small, proving the sentence. Nothing competes with the claim.
The move to borrowThe section header is the conclusion, written as a sentence: “Employment looks weaker than the headline suggests” — not “Employment.” Two ranks that never merge: a display CLAIM line containing no digits, and a mono MEASUREMENT line beneath it.

Paperpillar — “TIME PASSED / THE PASSION DIDN'T”Seen
Dribbble · search: magazine editorial layout
An oversized two-clause headline where the second clause reverses the first.
The move to borrowThat construction is literally our story's grammar: “X improved. Y didn't.” A standing headline form for every MIXED or DISTORTED signal — memorable, repeatable weekly, needs no economics. Guard against the failure mode: if a robot templates this sentence every week it degrades into mad-libs within a month. Write it by hand or don't ship it.
Tom Birch — dark editorial spreadsSeen
Dribbble · search: magazine editorial layout
Near-black pages, white multi-column type, contents set as a typographic index. No decoration whatsoever.
The move to borrowProof that dark + typographic reads as publication rather than terminal. The difference is column measure and leading — dashboards pack, publications breathe. Ooze has the palette already; it needs the spacing.
B

Instrumentation — disagreement as a designed state

Engineering solved this problem decades ago while design largely didn't. On-brand for a containment lab.
★ Aviation: the FLAG / DISAGREE splitConvention
Transport-aircraft primary flight displays — comparator monitors (IAS DISAGREE, ALT DISAGREE, AOA DISAGREE) vs failure flags
Two deliberately different grammars for two different failures. DISAGREE: both readings stay fully displayed and unchanged; a line of type appears in the gutter between them; no alarm, no flashing. FLAG: when no valid source exists, the number is removed — blanked or dashed — and a word occupies the space where the numeral was.
The move to borrow — and the refinement that matters MIXED and DISTORTED keep every number fully visible and add an annunciation in the gap between the two contradicting figures. UNCERTAIN does the opposite: delete the numeral, keep the frame and label, put a word in the hole. Every dashboard instead tints or greys the number, which communicates neither doubt nor confidence and just looks broken. Publish a threshold and a dwell requirement — “differs for two consecutive prints” — so DISAGREE can't fire on noise.
The move, in Ooze's language
HEADLINE
4.1%
DISAGREE
UNDERLYING
−23,000
Neither number is hidden, dimmed, or corrected. The disagreement itself is the published finding.
★ ntpq -p — the falseticker markFetched
docs.ntpsec.org · the tally column
A monospace table of time sources, one per row. The first column is a single character encoding that source's standing against the computed consensus: x = falseticker (disagrees with the majority), * = the system peer, + = combined. Consensus is computed first, then every source is marked in place.
The move to borrowFour labour indicators as four fixed-width rows; compute what the majority say; mark each row's standing in a one-glyph left gutter. The contradiction becomes a vertical scan — three rows one mark, one row another — read in about a second, with no chart and no colour. “Falseticker” is a real term of art the editorial voice can legitimately quote, and it teaches the mechanism for free.
The move, in Ooze's language
xunemployment rate4.1%
+nonfarm payrolls−23,000
+participation61.4%
+employment-population58.9%
x — this source disagrees with the other three about which way the labour market moved.
Nagios UNKNOWN and Grafana No Data — knowledge is not severityFetched
Nagios plugin exit codes · Grafana Alerting state model
Nagios defines UNKNOWN as exit code 3 — numerically past CRITICAL but explicitly not a worse severity: it means the check failed, not the thing. Grafana makes it structural, with No Data and Error as states separate from Alerting, routed independently.
The move to borrowSeverity and knowledge are two independent dimensions, and “we don't know” must never sit as a point on the severity ramp. Concretely: our stylesheet keys severity off [data-level="1"–"5"], so epistemic state needs a separate attribute rather than a level 6. That one decision keeps “uncertain” from reading as “extremely bad.”
Nikita Iarygin — alert builder with an expected bandSeen
Dribbble · search: anomaly detection
A line chart with a shaded expected range behind it; the series runs inside the band, then breaks out, and the breakout is the entire message.
The move to borrowDraw what we expected beside what happened. Unemployment falling is only strange because payrolls normally rise with it — so draw the expectation and the violation as one object.
AppSignal — “Triggered Alerts” as a word listSeen
Dribbble · search: anomaly detection
A plain text list of alerts with word-states — Warning, Ongoing, Cooling down, Ended — almost no colour and no charts. It reports problems while staying calm.
The move to borrowA word taxonomy instead of a colour taxonomy. Our three states can be carried by typography and position alone, which is also the accessibility answer.
C

Explanatory — draw the hidden variable

The “ohhh” moment is the deliverable. Also the only family that handles the oil case.
The ghost line and the wedgeVerified via author's own writeup
Bloomberg, “What's Really Warming the World” (2015) · NYT Upshot “You Draw It” (2015)
Bloomberg pins the observed line so it never leaves, then draws candidate explanations against it until one locks on. NYT removes the data entirely, has you draw your guess, then snaps the truth in beside your wrong line and leaves both on screen.
The move to borrowPin the observed thing; draw the expectation against it in the same units; hatch the wedge between them. The wedge is the people who left the workforce — the hidden variable becomes drawn rather than explained. It generalises with no new vocabulary: CONFIRMED = no wedge, MIXED = wedge, DISTORTED = wedge pointing the flattering way. Two more disciplines worth stealing: kill the legend and label each line at its own end, and label reference lines by their meaning (“if the jobs market were actually improving”) rather than their maths.
★ Analysis of Competing Hypotheses + ICD 203Primary docs
Heuer, Psychology of Intelligence Analysis (CIA, free online) · ODNI Intelligence Community Directive 203
ACH is a matrix: hypotheses across the top, evidence down the side, cells marked consistent or inconsistent. Its key concept is diagnosticity — evidence consistent with every hypothesis has zero value no matter how compelling it feels. ICD 203 is the binding analytic standard for US intelligence products and requires that likelihood be kept separate from confidence, and that information, assumption and judgement be visibly distinguished.
The move to borrow — this is the one that solves oil Present two named hypotheses as equals, list the same evidence under both, then state which observation would actually separate them. It reads rigorous rather than confused. And it hands DISTORTED a principled definition instead of an editorial opinion: a headline consistent with both hypotheses is non-diagnostic — it is telling you nothing while appearing to tell you something.
The move, in Ooze's language — the oil case

Cheaper fuel genuinely lowers household pressure. This is real, not a caveat.

The price alone cannot separate these. What would: freight volumes, refinery margins, inventories. Until we have one, the honest state is two readings, not a verdict.
Bret Victor — the reactive documentFetched
worrydream.com · “Explorable Explanations” (2011) and Tangle
Prose containing adjustable numbers inline — drag a number inside a sentence and the consequences update in the surrounding clauses.
The move to borrowPut the variable inside the sentence, not in a widget beside it: “If the ⟨0⟩ people who left the labour force had kept looking, unemployment would read 4.1%.” Drag it and the rate climbs past 4.2%. Whole mechanism, one line, no jargon. Hard constraint: the sentence must already be true and complete at its default value. A slider you must drag to discover the point is a point you failed to write.
Avnish Poonia — anomalies marked in placeSeen
Dribbble · search: anomaly detection
Offending cells are highlighted inside the spreadsheet rather than extracted into a separate panel, so outlier and context are seen together.
The move to borrowDon't exile the contradiction to a sidebar. Mark it on the ledger where the indicator already lives, so the reader sees which line is suspect while looking at all of them.
D

Encoding law — the rules every mockup is graded against

Not a direction of its own. This family exists to stop the other three producing something pretty and wrong.
★ Re-polarisation: the contradiction is a shape, not a colourComputed
Slopegraph form (Tufte) · FT Visual Vocabulary “Deviation” family · verified against our own July data
Two time points, one shared axis, one line per series, no legend and no fills. The pre-attentive signal is lines crossing.
The move to borrow — the single most important item on this board All four employment indicators fell, so on a raw axis they read as agreement. Flip unemployment's sign onto a shared “toward stress” axis and one line leaves the pack. The contradiction becomes a shape, in one hue, readable in about a second — and it survives on near-black, which most chart forms do not.
WCAG luminance — and a real bug in our own stylesheetRecomputed
WCAG relative-luminance formula, run against our literal tokens
Against our actual --bg:#070b06: --green scores 15.23:1 and --red scores 6.02:1. Green is 2.81× the luminance of red on our canvas. Green screams; red mutters. Separately, WCAG 1.4.1 forbids colour as the sole carrier of information.
What this forces — and what it caughtHue may never carry state alone, and two states that must be distinguished should be luminance-matched so neither outranks the other by sheer brightness. It also caught a live defect: lab.css:115 and lab.css:490 both set .down{color:var(--green)} — down is coded as good, in two separate components. For unemployment that happens to be right. For payrolls, participation and employment-share it is exactly backwards, and it is the same mistake our algorithm made, sitting in our CSS.
The dark monitoring dashboard, as a genreSeen
Dribbble · search: monitoring dashboard dark — an entire page of them
Rows of equal-sized KPI tiles and small line charts, every metric at the same visual weight.
Why it fails our problemMetric equality. If everything is the same size, nothing tells you where to look — and our whole job is to say “look here, this one is lying.” A layout that treats all seven Ooze lines identically cannot express that.

The white space: nobody has designed thisSeen
Dribbble · search: comparison chart annotation
Searching explicitly for annotated comparison returned almost entirely generic dashboards and slide templates — side-by-side columns comparing products, not two measurements of one thing that disagree.
Why this matters more than it looksThe design field has largely not solved this, while instrumentation engineering has. That is the case for taking Family B seriously — and it means whatever we build here is genuinely distinctive rather than a borrowed pattern.

The four directions I'd build

DirectionBuilt fromWhy it earns a slot
The Instrument RowB1 DISAGREE + B2 falseticker + D2 re-polarisation Monospace rows, one gutter glyph, no chart. Reads in a second, degrades gracefully to email, and makes “these instruments disagree” a permanent published state rather than a paragraph.
The Definition PageA1 OWID + C2 the U-1…U-6 ladder Maximum honesty and zero new vocabulary — we publish the agency's own definition and its own ladder. Hardest to argue with, and the least likely to age badly.
The Ghost & the WedgeC1 ghost line + C3 reactive sentence The only direction that shows why rather than asserting it. Highest risk of becoming a chart nobody reads — which is exactly why it needs a mockup rather than an argument.
Competing ReadingsC4 ACH + ICD 203 The only direction that handles oil, where one indicator tells two true stories. Gives DISTORTED a principled definition instead of an editorial opinion.
The biggest risk in this exploration That “What doesn't add up?” becomes a component rather than the product's identity. As a card it will be empty most weeks and read as filler — most months genuinely have no contradiction, and manufacturing one to fill the slot would be the worst possible outcome for a publication whose entire differentiator is not overstating what it knows. As an identity, the honest empty state — “Nothing contradicts this week. The signals agree.” — is itself a useful weekly message, and gives the product a reason to exist between monthly seals. Every mockup must show its empty state, and it will be judged as hard as the loud one.
