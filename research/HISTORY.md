## 2. Deep profiles — the five worst

### 🥇 The Great Depression (Aug 1929 – Mar 1933) — retro-Ooze: 100, sustained
- **Key dates:** Black Thursday Oct 24, 1929; Black Tuesday Oct 29, 1929; bank-run waves 1930–33; national bank holiday Mar 6, 1933.
- **Damage:** unemployment ~25%; real GDP −26%; Dow −89% (Sep 1929 peak 381 → Jul 1932 trough 41, not recovered until 1954); ~9,000 bank failures 1930–33; prices fell ~25% (deflation); ~1 in 5 mortgages in default by 1933.
- **Intake lines maxed:** employment, credit, housing, manufacturing — everything. The only event where the jar overflows for **years**.
- **Data note:** monthly unemployment before 1948 is reconstructed (Lebergott/Romer estimates); CPI exists from 1913; industrial production from 1919. A pre-1948 backfill is estimate-based and must be labeled as such.

### 🥈 The Great Financial Crisis (Dec 2007 – Jun 2009) — retro-Ooze: mid-90s peak (Oct 2008 – mid 2009)
- **Key dates:** housing prices peak mid-2006; New Century bankruptcy Apr 2, 2007; BNP Paribas freeze Aug 9, 2007 (credit crunch begins); Bear Stearns sold Mar 16, 2008; Fannie/Freddie conservatorship Sep 7, 2008; **Lehman Brothers bankruptcy Sep 15, 2008**; AIG rescue Sep 16; TARP Oct 3; unemployment peaks 10.0% Oct 2009.
- **Damage:** S&P 500 −57% (Oct 2007 – Mar 2009); Case-Shiller home prices −27%; ~2.9M foreclosure filings in 2010 alone (RealtyTrac), ~3.8M completed foreclosures 2007–2010; household net worth −~$16T; credit card charge-offs >10% (2010); auto repossessions ~1.9M (2009); PMI 33.1 (Dec 2008).
- **Intake lines:** housing and credit ruptured first (2006–07), employment followed with a lag — the exact cascade OOZEMeter's weighting is designed to show.

### 🥉 COVID-19 shock (Feb – Apr 2020) — retro-Ooze: mid-90s spike, fast recovery
- **Key dates:** S&P peak Feb 19, 2020; fastest-ever 30% drawdown (33 days) into Mar 23; national emergency Mar 13; initial claims hit **6.87M in one week** (Mar 28, ~10× the pre-2020 record); unemployment 14.8% Apr 2020.
- **Damage:** 22M jobs lost in two months; GDP −31% annualized in Q2 2020; oil futures briefly **negative** (Apr 20, 2020).
- **Shape:** vertical spike, vertical recovery — the jar overflows and drains faster than any event in history. Good stress test for the meter's responsiveness.

### 4. The Volcker double-dip (Jan 1980 – Nov 1982) — retro-Ooze: high 80s
- **Key dates:** CPI peaks 14.8% Mar 1980; fed funds ~20% (1981); unemployment 10.8% Nov–Dec 1982 (worst between 1940 and 2009); 30-yr mortgage rates peak **18.6%** (Oct 1981).
- **Why it matters for us:** the only era where the *inflation* and *housing-rate* lines, not employment, led the stress — the 2022–23 rhyme. Backfilling this era validates the weighting against a non-2008-shaped crisis.

### 5. Stagflation / oil embargo (Nov 1973 – Mar 1975) — retro-Ooze: low 80s
- **Key dates:** OPEC embargo Oct 1973 – Mar 1974; gas prices +~40%, odd-even rationing lines; S&P −48% (Jan 1973 – Oct 1974); CPI ~12.3% in 1974; unemployment 9.0% May 1975.
- **Why it matters:** proof the gas-price line deserves real weight — the era when the number on the sign genuinely drove national stress.
