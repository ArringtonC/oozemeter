Lesson 12 · DCOILWTICO

🛢 Energy

WTI is an upstream crude-oil benchmark. It affects refiners, transport, and inflation expectations, but it is not the retail gasoline price households pay.

Tangible win: Explain what this gauge measures, what it cannot prove, and reproduce the exact Ward M input.

1 · Know the measurement

Published input
DCOILWTICO

Daily Cushing, Oklahoma West Texas Intermediate spot price in dollars per barrel, aggregated to a monthly mean.

Release clock
Published by the U.S. Energy Information Administration

Ward M retains the observation period and collection time.

2 · Interpret it without overclaiming

Read the level together with duration, product markets, refinery conditions, taxes, distribution costs, and the gasoline line in the household jar. Pass-through is incomplete and occurs with lags.

Limits:

The current anchors are nominal dollars. Inflation changes what a fixed dollar price means over decades, and WTI can diverge from other crude benchmarks or retail fuels. Falling WTI can accompany collapsing demand during a recession, so a one-direction high-price scale can miss severe demand stress. The anchor-validation report therefore treats energy anchors as provisional evidence, not settled truth.

3 · Reproduce the data path

  1. Retrieve daily DCOILWTICO observations from EIA or FRED transport.
  2. Discard missing days and calculate the monthly arithmetic mean.
  3. Apply the published nominal-dollar anchors and label that basis explicitly.
  4. For anchor research, compare nominal and CPI-adjusted histories without silently replacing the released method.

4 · Compare with 2008

Historical basis:

Historical incident files use the latest data available at retrieval: a current-vintage reconstruction, not the release-time view available in 2008. Revised series can change, and a terminal calendar month may be partial.

WTI surged to record nominal levels before collapsing during the financial crisis. The same year demonstrated both inflationary pressure and demand destruction, so price direction requires context.
