Lesson 09 · T10Y3M

🏛 Rates

T10Y3M is a cycle-warning measure, not a contemporaneous stress meter. A negative spread is called an inversion; it reflects restrictive short rates relative to longer-run expectations, not a recession countdown clock.

Tangible win: Explain what this gauge measures, what it cannot prove, and reproduce the exact Ward M input.

1 · Know the measurement

Published input
T10Y3M

Monthly mean of the 10-year Treasury constant-maturity yield minus the 3-month Treasury constant-maturity yield, in percentage points. Treasury constant-maturity rates are modeled par yields, not transactions in one security.

Release clock
Daily market observations aggregated to a monthly mean by Ward M.

Ward M retains the observation period and collection time.

2 · Interpret it without overclaiming

Read the sign, depth, duration, and subsequent re-steepening together. Inversion has often preceded U.S. recessions, but the lead varies and an expansion can continue for many months. A positive spread can also return because short rates fall as conditions deteriorate, so “uninverted” is not automatically “safe.”

Limits:

The spread is a probabilistic leading signal, most useful months or quarters ahead. Policy regimes, term premiums, asset purchases, inflation expectations, and safe-haven demand can move either maturity. It has produced false or very early warnings, including a persistent 2022–24 inversion not followed by an NBER-dated recession as of July 2026. A positive spread during a crisis can reflect emergency easing, so this gauge is not contemporaneous crisis severity.

3 · Reproduce the data path

  1. Download T10Y3M observations from FRED or retrieve the component Treasury rates from their original publishers.
  2. Group valid daily observations by calendar month and calculate the arithmetic mean.
  3. Apply the published Ward M anchors; retain the observation month and retrieval timestamp.
  4. Compare the result with the full-history percentile report before proposing any anchor change.

4 · Compare with 2008

Historical basis:

Historical incident files use the latest data available at retrieval: a current-vintage reconstruction, not the release-time view available in 2008. Revised series can change, and a terminal calendar month may be partial.

The curve inverted before the 2007–09 recession, then steepened sharply as the Federal Reserve cut short rates. The inversion was useful as an earlier warning; the positive spread during the crisis was not evidence that the crisis had passed.
