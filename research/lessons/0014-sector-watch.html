Lesson 14 · SECTOR-BREADTH

📊 Sector Watch

Sector Watch asks whether weakness is narrow or broad. It uses recognizable ticker proxies so a reader can verify each row, but an ETF is not the economy or the sector itself.

Tangible win: Explain what this gauge measures, what it cannot prove, and reproduce the exact Ward M input.

1 · Know the measurement

Published input
SECTOR-BREADTH

An 11-ticker equity breadth proxy panel classified by price return: steady at −2% or better, softening from −7% inclusive to below −2%, and stressed below −7%. It mixes four broad/style indexes, five S&P 500 sectors, transportation, and semiconductors; it is not eleven independent sectors.

Release clock
Collected manually from the undocumented Yahoo Finance chart endpoint while quote rights remain unresolved. Only ticker, role, derived percentage changes, state, and date are published

Ward M retains the observation period and collection time.

2 · Interpret it without overclaiming

Read each ticker’s state and the count across all eleven. Ward M breadth gives half weight to softening rows and full weight to stressed rows before mapping the weakness share to stress anchors.

Limits:

The collector uses quote.close, a price-return field that is not a total-return series; distributions can make a ticker appear weaker. The live gauge uses a 22-session daily interval; the research backtest uses successive monthly closes, so historical breadth is a non-identical reconstruction. Fixed thresholds favor volatile funds, all tickers receive one vote, and SPY, QQQ, SMH, and sector funds overlap. Quote rights remain unresolved, so scheduled publishing is disabled pending a licensed or explicitly permitted source.

3 · Reproduce the data path

  1. Retrieve at least 65 valid quote.close observations for each published ticker and record provider, exchange date, timezone, and corporate-action policy.
  2. Calculate 22-session and 64-session intervals from the latest valid close using the closes 22 and 64 sessions earlier; do not call them calendar-month or total returns.
  3. Apply exact boundaries: steady at or above −2%; softening from −7% inclusive to below −2%; stressed below −7%.
  4. Reconcile 11 valid rows, then compute weakness share as (0.5 × softening + stressed) ÷ 11. Label historical monthly-close results as a research reconstruction.

5 · Compare with 2008

Historical basis:

Historical incident files use the latest data available at retrieval: a current-vintage reconstruction, not the release-time view available in 2008. Revised series can change, and a terminal calendar month may be partial.

During the financial crisis weakness spread across broad indexes, financials, industrials, transports, consumers, innovation, and defensive sectors. Broad participation distinguishes systemic repricing from an isolated industry shock.
