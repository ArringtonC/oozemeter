Lesson 10 · VIXCLS

🌪 Volatility

VIX uses S&P 500 option prices to estimate the market’s annualized expectation of 30-day volatility. It reflects a risk-neutral distribution and volatility risk premium, not an unbiased forecast or the expected direction of stocks.

Tangible win: Explain what this gauge measures, what it cannot prove, and reproduce the exact Ward M input.

1 · Know the measurement

Published input
VIXCLS

Cboe VIX closing level, aggregated by Ward M to a monthly mean of valid daily closes.

Release clock
Cboe calculates VIX during trading hours

Ward M retains the observation period and collection time.

2 · Interpret it without overclaiming

VIX reflects a risk-neutral distribution and volatility risk premium. Separate a one-day shock from persistent repricing: a spike can mark acute demand for protection, while a high monthly mean indicates follow-through. A very low reading can mean genuine stability or inexpensive insurance; “complacency” requires evidence VIX alone does not provide.

Limits:

VIX is equity-option-implied volatility, not an economy-wide fear meter. It is affected by option supply and demand, hedging flows, event calendars, and the averaging window. Monthly means deliberately mute brief intramonth spikes. FRED can lag the same-day Cboe close, so the observation date—not retrieval time alone—must be shown. Cboe marks the series copyrighted; FRED transport does not make it public domain.

3 · Reproduce the data path

  1. Retrieve VIXCLS daily closes from FRED and retain Cboe as the original publisher.
  2. Discard missing observations and calculate the arithmetic mean for each calendar month.
  3. Apply the published volatility anchors to the monthly mean.
  4. Preserve the Cboe methodology link, observation month, and retrieval timestamp.

4 · Compare with 2008

Historical basis:

Historical incident files use the latest data available at retrieval: a current-vintage reconstruction, not the release-time view available in 2008. Revised series can change, and a terminal calendar month may be partial.

VIX reached extraordinary levels during the financial crisis and remained elevated, unlike many short-lived event spikes. The persistence—not merely the maximum print—distinguished system-wide repricing.
