# Mission: Understand and Defend the OOZEMeter Intake Lines

## Why
Build enough economic and data-source fluency to explain, implement, audit, and publicly defend every area that feeds OOZEMeter. The lessons should turn each intake line from a decorative label into an understood measurement system with a trustworthy acquisition path.

## Success looks like
- Explain what each of the eight intake areas measures and why it matters to household economic stress.
- Identify the primary metric, backup metrics, source owner, series identifier, units, cadence, lag, and revision behavior for every area.
- Distinguish a real measurement from a proxy and label each honestly in the product.
- Inspect a new release and judge whether it raises, lowers, or does not change that line's stress.
- Trace every displayed value from OOZEMeter back to a reproducible first-party source.

## Constraints
- Primary and first-party sources outrank commentary and news coverage.
- Lessons must be short, interactive HTML documents that share reusable assets.
- Launch data should be free or very low cost; paid/licensed series must be clearly separated from launch proxies.
- The scoring model measures household economic stress; it does not forecast recessions.
- The eight areas are Gas Prices, Housing, Credit Cards, Auto Loans, Unemployment, Inflation, Foreclosures, and Manufacturing.

## Out of scope
- Trading or investment recommendations.
- Building a full economics degree curriculum.
- Changing production weights or anchors before the lessons expose the evidence and trade-offs.
- Presenting paid or reconstructed data as a live first-party measurement.
