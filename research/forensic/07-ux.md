## 0. Headline verdict

**The site is trustworthy about its arithmetic and untrustworthy about its conclusion.**

Every number rendered on OOZEMeter traces to a public series. The integrity gate works. The methodology page
publishes weights, calibration, bands, and the OOZEMAXING breadth rule. The zero-weight firewall is restated
everywhere it should be. As a *measurement instrument*, this is better disclosed than most paid products.

But the product answers four of the user's five questions and the fifth is the one that matters.

| The user asks | The site answers | Where |
|---|---|---|
| Is it getting better or worse? | **Yes, well.** `26/100`, `▼ −1`, `Calmer than 6 of every 10 months since 2003` | `index.html:37–42` |
| What changed? | **Partially.** Four "canister" cards — which this month show the *wrong four lines* (§5.2) | `index.html:196–204` |
| Why? | **Not on the page.** The answer is generated, is Constitution-compliant, and is never rendered on the homepage (§5.1) | `data/editorial.js` `story` |
| Does this affect me? | **Weakly.** One un-rendered household paragraph; one simulated-data calculator | `personal.html` |
| What should I watch? | **Buried.** `specimen-progress.html` is a link in a rail, not an answer | `index.html:93` |

And the load-bearing failure:

> **For July 2026 the site's plain-English household paragraph tells the reader
> *"steady employment kept paychecks coming."*
> In July 2026 payrolls fell, household employment fell, the labor force fell by a quarter of a million people,
> and the share of adults in the workforce hit 61.4% — a level exceeded in 280 of the 282 months in the jar's
> own comparable record.**
>
> The unemployment rate fell *because* people left the count. The jar reads the unemployment rate. It cannot
> see the exit. It therefore reported relief, and the editorial engine — faithfully, correctly, deterministically
> — translated that relief into a sentence of reassurance about the one thing that deteriorated.

This is not an editorial failure. The prose is exactly as good as its input. It is a **product** failure: the
site has no surface whose job is to say *"the instrument and the world disagree this month."* That surface is
what §4 of this report designs.

## 1. The July 2026 false positive — verified against FRED

All series fetched `2026-08-13` from `https://fred.stlouisfed.org/graph/fredgraph.csv?id=<ID>`.

| Series | Jun 2026 | Jul 2026 | Change | Direction |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `UNRATE` — unemployment rate | 4.2% | **4.1%** | −0.1 pt | Looks better |
| `PAYEMS` — nonfarm payrolls (thousands) | 158,881 | **158,858** | −23 | Worse |
| `CE16OV` — household employment (thousands) | 162,264 | **162,177** | −87 | Worse |
| `CLF16OV` — labor force (thousands) | 169,358 | **169,094** | −264 | Worse |
| `UNEMPLOY` — unemployed (thousands) | 7,094 | **6,916** | −178 | Ambiguous |
| `CIVPART` — participation rate | 61.5% | **61.4%** | −0.1 pt | Worse |
| `EMRATIO` — employment-population ratio | 59.0% | **58.9%** | −0.1 pt | Worse |
| `U6RATE` — broad underemployment | 7.9% | 7.9% | 0.0 | Flat |

**The arithmetic of the contradiction.** The labor force fell 264,000. Of that, 178,000 left the *unemployed*
count and 87,000 left the *employed* count. Because the unemployment rate is `unemployed ÷ labor force`, a
shrinking denominator lowered the rate while both of its inputs got smaller.

**Counterfactual, computed:** hold the labor force at June's 169,358,000 and take July's actual employment of
162,177,000. Unemployed = 7,181,000. Rate = **4.24%**, which publishes as **4.2%** — the same as June.
So: *had nobody left the workforce, the rate would have read 4.2%, not 4.1%.* The entire published improvement
is denominator.

**Twelve-month framing (less noisy, therefore stronger).** `EMRATIO` was 59.6% in July 2025 and is 58.9% now.
Applying July 2025's ratio to July 2026's civilian noninstitutional population (`CNP16OV` = 275,282,000) yields
164,068,000 employed against an actual 162,177,000 — a gap of **about 1.9 million people**.

**Percentile placement against the jar's own 2003-present comparable window (282 months):**
- `CIVPART` 61.4% — lower in **2 of 282** months (0.7th percentile).
- `EMRATIO` 58.9% — lower in **72 of 282** months (25.5th percentile).

**Honesty correction to my own finding.** A single month's −23,000 payroll change is *inside* the establishment
survey's own sampling error (BLS publishes roughly ±136,000 at 90% confidence for the monthly change). Any
module built on this must **not** lead with the one-month payroll wiggle. The robust, low-noise evidence is the
participation and employment-population trend. I have written §4's copy accordingly. **Claim type:
SUPPORTED-EXPLANATION** for the labor-force-exit mechanism (the arithmetic is closed and exact);
**CORRELATION only** for any suggestion that this pattern implies what comes next — and the Constitution
forbids saying that anyway (§5, *never predict*).

**Why the jar cannot see it** (`scripts/collect.js:108`):

```js
jobs: Math.max(interp(ANCHORS.unemployment, un), interp(ANCHORS.claimsK, icsa / 1000)),
```

Both branches improved in July. `UNRATE` 4.1 and `ICSA` four-week mean near 200,000 both sit at the calm end of
their anchor tables (`scripts/collect.js:38–39`), which is why `jobs.stress = 13` and `jobs.contrib = 2` — the
heaviest-weighted line in the formula (24.25%) is contributing the second-fewest ounces in the jar. This is the
documented employment-signal defect (`research/EMPLOYMENT-SIGNAL-DECISION-2026-08-11.md`) and I am **not**
re-reporting it as new. What *is* new here is the product consequence: **the defect is currently producing a
reassuring sentence on the most-read surface of the site, and nothing on the site contradicts it.**

## 3. The progressive explanation ladder

**Design rule.** A normal user must reach a correct conclusion at Level 2 and never need Level 5. An expert must
reach Level 5 and be able to audit the instrument *including the case where the instrument is wrong*. Each level
is one interaction deeper. **No level may contradict a level above it.**

Today the site ships **L1, half of L2, and a jump to L5.** L3 exists as generated text and is not rendered.
L4 does not exist. That gap is why a reader can leave the July page believing employment is steady.

### LEVEL 1 — the reading (always visible, above the fold)

```
                    Containment Level — July 2026

                            26/100
                    STICKY      ▼ −1 vs June 2026
          Calmer than 6 of every 10 months since 2003
```

Ships today, correctly (`index.html:37–42`). **No change.** This is the 15-second promise of §2 and it is kept.

### LEVEL 2 — What's driving it? (visible, no click)

Replace the four mover-sorted canisters with **the three heaviest lines by ounces, plus the month's largest
mover**, and print the ounces on the face of each card.

```
  WHAT'S DRIVING IT                                    26 oz total

  🏠 Housing        7 oz    30-yr mortgage 6.67%        was flat
  💳 Credit Cards   6 oz    card delinquency 2.9%       was flat
  🚗 Auto Loans     5 oz    auto delinquency 7.9%       was flat
  ⛽ Gas Prices     4 oz    pump price $4.01            eased 3 points
                                                  ┌──────────────────┐
  8 oz across the three lighter lines →           │ See all 9 lines →│
                                                  └──────────────────┘
```

Every card carries **a name, its ounces, its observed value, and a §3-ladder verb** — which satisfies §4
(*no line named without the observed value that produced its score, in the same sentence*) at the card level
rather than only in prose. The ounces sum to the printed whole (§12 fraction sweep).

### LEVEL 3 — What changed? (visible, no click — this is the missing homepage block)

Render `EDITORIAL.story` and the household paragraph **on the homepage**, under the canisters. Both already

### LEVEL 4 — What doesn't add up? (visible, no click — does not exist; designed in §4)

Series IDs, both instruments' values, dates, the transform, the anchor pair used, and the arithmetic —

### Ladder integrity test

Give a stranger only L1+L2+L3 for July 2026 and ask *"how is the job market?"*
- **Today** they answer *"steady — it says so"* and they are wrong.
- **With L4 present** they answer *"unemployment looks better but fewer people are working, and the site says

That single sentence is the entire return on this design.

## 4. The "WHAT DOESN'T ADD UP?" module

### 4.1 What it is

A permanent homepage block, directly below Level 3, that runs a fixed, published set of cross-checks between
**the series the jar reads** and **a named series the jar does not read**, and reports the result — including
when the result is "nothing."

It is not a second opinion, not a forecast, and not a confidence interval. It is a **disagreement register**.
It exists because the jar is seven numbers and the economy is not, and because the Constitution's own §15 says
*"A correction is not an embarrassment to be minimized; it is the strongest evidence the facility is what it
says it is."* The same logic applies before an error is a correction.

### 4.2 The checks — published, fixed, reproducible

Each weighted line gets one cross-check series and one stated contradiction rule. Published in `notes.html`
next to the anchor tables, exactly as Ward M publishes its breadth formula.

| Line | Jar reads | Cross-check (not scored) | Contradiction fires when |
|---|---|---|---|
| Employment | `UNRATE`, `ICSA` | `PAYEMS`, `CIVPART`, `EMRATIO` | line stress falls while `EMRATIO` falls, **or** `CIVPART` falls ≥0.3 pt over 12 months |
| Housing | `MORTGAGE30US`, `DRSFRMACBS` | `HOUST` | mortgage rate eases while starts fall ≥10% over 12 months |
| Credit | `DRCCLACBS` | `REVOLSL`, `TERMCBCCALLNS` | delinquency falls while balances **and** APR both climb |
| Auto | NY Fed 30+ transition | `TOTALSA` | delinquency falls while unit sales fall ≥5% over 12 months |
| Gas | `GASREGW` | `DCOILWTICO` | pump and crude move in opposite directions over 3 months |
| Inflation | `CPIAUCNS` YoY | `CPILFESL` YoY | headline eases while core climbs |
| Financial | `NFCI` | `ANFCI` | the two indices disagree in sign |

**Three states, defined by count, not by feel:**

- 🟢 **Signals broadly agree** — zero contradictions fire.
- 🟡 **Mixed signals** — one contradiction fires.
- 🔴 **Meaningful contradiction detected** — two or more fire, **or** any single one fires on the employment
  line. Employment gets the escalation because it carries 24.25% of the formula and, per §7,
  *"Recessions are employment events."*

**No-data sentence** (§6, *a section is never published empty*):
*"Cross-checks could not run this month — one or more comparison series has not released. A missing month
stays missing."*

### 4.3 The copy — July 2026, red state

Rendered under 30 seconds, no jargon, no intensifiers, no forecast, no bare score points, every named line
carrying its observed value.

```
  ┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
  │  🔴  WHAT DOESN'T ADD UP?          Meaningful contradiction detected│
  ├────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
  │                                                                    │
  │  Two job numbers point opposite ways this month.                   │
  │                                                                    │
  │  The unemployment rate fell to 4.1%. Over the same month the        │
  │  share of American adults with a job fell to 58.9%, and the share   │
  │  either working or looking fell to 61.4%.                          │
  │                                                                    │

  │  The jar reads the unemployment rate. It does not read the number   │
```

**Word count 128. Reading time ~28 seconds. Grade-8 vocabulary throughout.**

Constitution check, clause by clause:
- **§3 verb ladder** — `fell`, `stopped looking`. No intensifier on any data verb. ✅
- **§4 observed values** — every named quantity carries its number in the same sentence. ✅

  reused here. ✅
- **§5 never sensationalize** — no siren, no "warning", no "recession". The state label is `Meaningful

- **§8 one governing idea in the first two paragraphs** — *two job numbers disagree*. ✅
- **§10 no hand-typed numbers** — see §4.4. ✅
- **§12 travel test** — the block is self-contained; screenshotted alone it still carries the zero-weight

## 6. Complexity I am rejecting

More data does not mean a better instrument. Things this audit deliberately does **not** recommend:

- **Adding PAYEMS to the score.** That is a methodology question and it belongs to the methodology agent, not
  to UX. The UX answer to a blind spot is **disclose it**, not silently patch it. A cross-check module that
  says "the jar cannot see this" is more trustworthy than a jar quietly rebuilt to see it.
- **A second composite / "true stress index."** Two numbers is zero numbers.
- **Per-line sparkline charts.** `INDICATORS[].spark` already exists as fabricated data and is already
  unrendered. Delete it; do not implement it.
- **Confidence intervals on the jar.** The reading is a deterministic transform of published series; an
  interval would imply a sampling model the instrument does not have.
- **Personalization beyond `personal.html`.** The product's value is that everyone reads the same number.
- **More cross-checks than one per line.** Seven checks, seven rules, one sentence. A disagreement register
  with 30 checks is a dashboard, and a dashboard is what this site exists not to be.
