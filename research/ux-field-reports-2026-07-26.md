# UX Field Reports — CNBC.com & Apple.com (clean-room agents, 2026-07-26)

Two agents with zero project context fetched and analyzed cnbc.com and apple.com.
Summarized here; commissioned to benchmark OOZEMeter's navigation and information access.

## CNBC — key findings
- Primary nav: 10 items + hamburger; every dropdown is exactly 2 levels deep — all ~70 destinations are one hover+click from anywhere.
- Market data lives in the CHROME: persistent tabbed ticker banner (US/ASIA/EUR/BONDS/OIL/GOLD/FX/CRYPTO) under the nav on every page, plus a hover "Market Movers" panel. Each ticker pairs with ONE editorial teaser headline.
- Dual-speed homepage: one curated hero package + an unillustrated 30-item timestamped "Latest News" rail. Both "what matters" and "what's new" answered with zero scroll.
- Nav becomes a bottom tab bar on mobile (app-like), sticky 45px on scroll.
- Dual timestamps everywhere (relative when fresh, absolute when old); "Trending Now" ranked list; QuickLinks chip row = 7 daily hot topics.
- Premium badged inline (small PRO pills on headlines) instead of walled sections. Only ~5 ad slots total.
- Article pages: eyebrow section label, KEY POINTS bullet summary above body, inline ticker chips, related rail.

## Apple — key findings
- Global nav capped at 11 nouns + search + bag. Every flyout = same formula: explore items / Compare / Shop. Learn the menu once, works everywhere.
- Product pages carry a sticky LOCAL sub-nav: product name left, chapter links (Tech Specs, Compare) + one always-visible Buy button right.
- Tile grammar: headline ≤4 words + one-sentence benefit + exactly two CTAs ("Learn more | Buy") over one hero image; alternate light/dark tiles for rhythm.
- One message per viewport; "Get the highlights" condensed module for skimmers before the long scroll.
- Price appears where INTENT is (after clicking Buy), not where attention is; complexity demoted to footnotes.
- Newsroom: flat reverse-chron cards (CATEGORY LABEL + date + headline), three dropdown filters (topic/year/month), scoped search — 253 pages of archive behind three controls.

## Ten transferable principles (merged)
1. Data layer in the chrome — the score/ticker reachable from every page without navigation. (CNBC)
2. Pair every ambient number with one editorial hook line. (CNBC)
3. Dual-speed homepage: one hero + a timestamped latest list. (CNBC)
4. Nav depth exactly two; everything one hover+click away. (CNBC)
5. Mobile nav as bottom tab bar. (CNBC)
6. Sticky local sub-nav on content pages with chapter links + one primary action. (Apple)
7. Fixed tile grammar: short headline + one benefit + two CTAs max. (Apple)
8. One message per viewport; a highlights module for skimmers. (Apple)
9. Same flyout formula in every menu so it's learned once. (Apple)
10. News as flat reverse-chron cards with category chips + three filters, no clever curation. (Apple)

## Gap #4 — Ads (added by operator question)
- CNBC HAS ads: only ~5 named slots on the whole homepage (TopBanner, sponsor logos INSIDE data modules, one mid-river in-feed, MobileAdhesion sticky, sponsored nav countdown clock). Restraint + structure. Premium sold via inline PRO pills on headlines, not walled sections.
- Apple has ZERO ads — cleanliness is the monetization; only self-promotion tiles; price demoted to footnotes.
- OOZEMeter doctrine: CNBC's ad model on Apple's surface budget. Max ~3 named slots, article pages only, never the Jar (law). Aspirational format: sponsor-logo-in-module — "this specimen sponsored by ___" etched on the beaker. Premium later = inline pill badges, not a walled wing.
