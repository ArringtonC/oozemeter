The Nine Sensors

What this ward is — and is not

Ward M is an experimental market-stress instrument, opened after the facility's market-signal review. It exists because financial conditions have historically led household pressure by months — the plumbing breaks before the kitchen sink stops working — but it also cries wolf: markets have panicked plenty of times while households stayed fine. So the ward is kept in a separate wing, on its own scale, with its own seal. Nothing here changes the Ooze Score.

The sensors were chosen by translating a market watchlist — sector funds, homebuilder stocks, industrial names, treasury yields — into their official public-data equivalents. Ticker and index prices can't be republished by a free public site (exchange and index licensing), and a government-measured series usually tells the same sector's story with cleaner provenance: the homebuilder stocks become Census building permits; the transports fund becomes the DOT freight index; the industrial names become Federal Reserve industrial production.

The composite is the plain average of the eight weighted sensors, and the calibration is provisional — anchors are published in data/market.json and the collector source, and they will be re-anchored once the ward has lived through a real tightening episode. The Speculative sensor (bitcoin drawdown) is auxiliary: watched, zero weight.

The full decision record — why the market is displayed beside the jar rather than blended into it, four backtested studies included — is public in the market-signal review. The Credit & Funding sensor (Chicago Fed NFCI) is the designated bridge for methodology v3, which will add it to the flagship at 3% under the disclosures recorded there.
