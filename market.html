What this ward is — and is not

Ward M is an experimental market-stress instrument, opened after the facility's market-signal review. Some financial conditions can move before household outcomes, but this same-month comparison does not establish a lead, forecast, or causal relationship. Markets also cry wolf: they have panicked plenty of times while households stayed fine. So the ward is kept in a separate wing, on its own scale, with its own seal. Nothing here changes the Ooze Score.

The composite averages six gauges — the bond market's recession signal, the stock market's fear gauge, a Federal Reserve credit index, oil, the dollar, and breadth from Sector Watch (so one weakening ticker proxy — a single corner of the market going soft — moves the score). It is calibrated the same way the jar is: the ward's calmest month since 2007 reads 10, its worst month of the 2008 crisis reads 90. The two instruments share no data and genuinely disagree when history disagrees — markets screamed through 2020 and late 2022 while households stayed comparatively calm. That disagreement is the reason this wing exists.

Sector Watch is the part anyone can check: eleven tickers you can look up anywhere, in seconds. It sits below the composite because it is the evidence, not the verdict. The exact sources, calibration constants, provisional-anchor status, and licensing fine print are all published in the Lab Notes annex — nothing here is a black box.

The full decision record — why the market is displayed beside the jar rather than blended into it, four backtested studies included — is public in the market-signal review.
