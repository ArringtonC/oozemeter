THE OOZE REPORT
August 4, 2026 — re-issue of the August 3 edition, corrected
====================================================

CORRECTION NOTICE
This edition replaces the edition of August 3, 2026, which is retained unchanged
in the archive. Two of its statements were wrong. It reported "breadth was
unchanged" when that change was never measured, and it reported June's score
without disclosing that June had been restated from 27 to 26 thirty-four hours
earlier under a new methodology. The August 3 edition was the only one affected
and it was current for one day. Nothing in the collector has been fixed yet: the
prior-cycle snapshot store that would let the facility measure a breadth change
is open work, listed publicly in the edition changelog. Full account at the end.

THE SEAL
Household OOZE stands at 26 out of 100 for June 2026 — that is Sticky territory,
the band where normal economies live — down 3 from May's restated 29. It has
taken no new monthly observation since the June seal and will take none until
July seals. It has, however, been restated once inside that window.

June was restated from 27 to 26 on August 1 under methodology 3.0.0, which added
a seventh weighted line, financial conditions, and rescaled the other six. The
restatement moved 180 of the 281 archived months by at most two points and
changed nine band labels. The full record is public in data/revisions.json.

THE VERDICT LINE
June is calmer than 6 of every 10 months since 2003 — measured against all 281
months in the record, not a chosen window.

WHAT MOVED
Inside the June seal, pressure from gas prices fell 11 line-stress points and
pressure from inflation fell 9 — the two largest moves on the board — while
employment eased 2 and housing edged up 1. Those are changes inside each
component's own 0-to-100 reading. They are not points subtracted from the
headline: gas has the highest line stress on the panel and the largest move, and
still supplies only 4 of the 26 contribution points.

Since that seal, three lines have posted newer observations than the month they
scored. The pump was $4.10 a gallon on July 27; the 30-year mortgage was 6.66%
on July 30; the Chicago Fed's financial-conditions index was −0.54 on July 24.
Those are the current levels. They are not the figures that produced June's
score, which was computed from June's monthly averages, and they will feed the
July seal instead.

This edition cannot tell you how far any level moved in the last seven days. The
facility stores month-over-month change and retains no week-over-week snapshot,
so where a number is a level it is labelled a level.

WHAT'S STILL PRESSING
Housing and card debt each supply 6 oz of the month's 26, with auto loans close
behind at 5 oz. The seven weighted lines split the 26 ounces between them and
sum to it exactly.

The two heaviest lines rest on very different evidence. Housing's 6 oz sit on a
mortgage rate whose newest print, 6.66%, is five days old. Card debt's 6 oz sit
on a delinquency rate of 2.9% observed on January 1, and auto loans' 5 oz on a
7.7% rate observed the same day — both report quarterly and neither has released
since. Those are the latest figures that exist, and 11 of the month's 26 ounces
rest on a January observation, which is worth knowing before anyone treats this
month's number as this month's news.

Employment supplies 3 oz — third-lightest of the seven, ahead of only inflation
at 2 oz and financial conditions at 0 oz — despite carrying the heaviest weight
in the formula, because unemployment at 4.2% on June 1 maps to a line stress of
14. Recessions are employment events. This board is not showing one.

WHAT A HOUSEHOLD WOULD NOTICE
Filling the tank cost $4.10 a gallon at the end of July. Anyone shopping for a
mortgage meets 6.66%. Anyone carrying a card balance is paying it against a
delinquency backdrop last measured in the winter, so the jar cannot yet say
whether that got better or worse this spring. Nothing a household meets in an
ordinary week changed in this week's data. What changed is what the facility
knows about June — it knows one point less than it did a week ago.

WARD M — THE MARKET WING
Ward M stands at 37 out of 100 on July evidence. It is an experimental
instrument and it contributes nothing to the household score above.

Four of its six gauges cooled: energy heat eased 7 points with WTI at $80 a
barrel, volatility heat eased 3 with the VIX monthly mean at 17.1, credit and
funding heat eased 2 with the Chicago Fed index at −0.54, and rates heat eased 1
with the 10-year minus 3-month spread at 0.73pp. Dollar heat edged up 3 with the
broad index 0.5% above a year ago. All five are July monthly means. As on the
household side, a gauge-heat move is a change inside that gauge's own 0-to-100
reading and is not subtracted from the composite: one gauge point moves Ward M
by roughly a quarter of a point.

The sixth gauge is breadth, and its change is not measured this cycle. Breadth
stands at 50 out of 100 — the highest of the six — on a panel of 6 steady, 4
softening and 1 stressed as of July 31. The collector compares against a file it
then overwrites, so the published change of zero is an artifact rather than an
observation, and the research reconstruction that would supply a real figure is
built on a different transform than the live gauge. The honest statement is that
breadth is the highest gauge on the panel and that the facility cannot yet say
by how much it moved.

TWO INSTRUMENTS, ONE MONTH
Ward 37 out of 100 against a household jar of 26 out of 100. The two are on
different clocks — the ward's gauges are July, the jar's seal is June — so that
is not a same-month comparison and should not be read as one. The last month
both instruments have a figure for is June 2026: ward 30, jar 26. Between May
and June the ward moved and the jar fell 3, so on the one month they share, the
market wing was carrying more stress than the kitchen table and moving
differently. For a household that means nothing directly: no market gauge
touches the household score, and a ward reading has never been evidence about a
grocery bill.

The instruments are never averaged. They overlap in one series: the Chicago Fed's
financial-conditions index. Ward M scores its July mean at −0.54; the household
jar scored June's mean, where the line carries 3% of the weight and 0 oz of the
26. One series, two months, two readings — which is why the two numbers on this
page are not the same number.

THE CLOSE
July's household specimen seals when the July jobs report and CPI land, the
earliest moment all seven lines exist for that month; the facility does not yet
carry the BLS release calendar as a checked fact, so this edition names the
event rather than the date. Ward M's gauges roll when their Federal Reserve,
Cboe and EIA series post. Sector Watch refreshes when the operator runs a
collection, which stays manual until quote-redistribution rights are cleared.
The jar updates itself; you just check it.

WHAT WAS CORRECTED FROM THE AUGUST 3 EDITION
1. "Breadth was unchanged" — withdrawn. That change was never measured. The
   collector diffs the payload against the file it is about to overwrite, so it
   produced a zero rather than an observation. Breadth is also the gauge that
   carried Ward M's July level; the August 3 account of "the official gauges
   cooled" therefore mis-stated the month's driver.
2. The June restatement — added. The August 3 edition reported 26 and a
   three-point fall without disclosing that both figures had been restated under
   a new methodology thirty-four hours earlier.
3. Observed values — added, and separated from the scores. The August 3 edition
   named seven household lines and six market gauges and printed the value of
   none. This edition prints them, with the date each was observed, and does not
   claim that a July print produced a June score.
4. "Household inputs current" — withdrawn. It was printed over two January
   observations supplying 11 of 26 ounces. Every line now carries its own date.
5. The verdict line, the seal's delta, and the household paragraph — restored.
   All three are required in every edition and all three were missing.

METHODOLOGY
Household OOZE summarizes measured household pressure under methodology 3.0.0,
from series published by the Bureau of Labor Statistics, Freddie Mac, the Federal
Reserve Bank of New York, the Energy Information Administration and the Federal
Reserve Bank of Chicago. Ward M summarizes market and financial-system stress on
its own scale from Federal Reserve, Cboe and EIA series; it does not affect the
household score, and its calibration anchors remain provisional. Component
line-stress changes and headline contribution ounces are different units. This
edition reports the latest validated evidence and does not forecast the economy.

Methodology v3.0.0. All source releases current at collection; two weighted lines
carry January observations by quarterly cadence. Two entries on the public
revision record (data/revisions.json), the most recent a methodology
recalibration. Every figure traces to a cited public series; anchors, weights and
calibration constants are published and frozen.
