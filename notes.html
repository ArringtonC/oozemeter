Restricted Wing — Lab Notes

The Ooze Formula

No black boxes in this facility. Every point of ooze is explainable and reproducible — this page is the complete recipe.

How the specimen is measured

OOZE = Σ ( weightᵢ × stressᵢ )

Each indicator is scored 0–100 against fixed, published anchor points (unemployment at 25% = 100; the calmest modern month = 10; the 2008 peak = 90), then multiplied by its weight. The month's contributions sum to the headline — currently {TOTAL} ({MONTH}).

Cadence: the headline seals once a month, when that month's jobs report and CPI land (~mid-month — the earliest moment all six lines exist). Individual intake lines refresh whenever their source releases: gas and mortgage rates weekly, jobs and inflation monthly, delinquencies quarterly. Each line carries its own as-of date, and a line gone quiet past its normal cadence is flagged STALE rather than silently reused.

Weights are fixed and published. Every chart links to its original government or industry source. If we change the recipe, we tell you.

The v3 change — a seventh line, and the bet behind it

In methodology v3 the jar gained a seventh intake line: Financial Conditions (3%), the Chicago Fed's 105-indicator NFCI. It is a bet, labeled as one: in backtesting it made the score climb about a month earlier in the slow credit tightening of 2007–08, and did nothing in the fast crash of 2020. Its measured benefit comes from that single historical episode — remove 2007–09 from the record and the benefit disappears — and in calm markets its arithmetic effect on the blended score is slightly negative. We weighted it at 3% because that is the smallest weight where the early-warning effect is visible and the largest where every score movement still has a household explanation. The incumbents scaled ×0.97 to make room; no line was singled out. The full decision record and all four backtested studies are public in the market-signal review.

Depression-class. Never met in the comparable record since 2003: at the GFC's worst (June 2009), five of six lines crossed 60 — credit 90, housing 87, autos 85, employment 78, gas 62 — and only deflation, holding the inflation line at 55, kept the declaration off the board.

Auxiliary sensors — on the board, not in the formula

Two additional lines appear in the ledger at zero weight: Foreclosures (proxied by the Fed's single-family mortgage delinquency rate, DRSFRMACBS) and Manufacturing (industrial production INDPRO plus manufacturers' new orders AMTMNO). They are labeled AUX everywhere they render and contribute nothing to the score — they ride along so the cascade's terminal stage and the factory floor stay visible while their proxies earn (or fail to earn) a weighted place. The complete recipe is the six weighted lines above plus these two disclosed passengers.

Annex — Ward M

The market wing's recipe

Ward M is a separate instrument on its own 0–100 scale. It never adds to or subtracts from the Ooze Score. The same house rules bind it: no simulated number ever renders without a label, and nothing here forecasts — the ward measures market conditions now; it does not predict recessions, returns, or the jar.

The composite averages six gauges — rates (10-year minus 3-month Treasury spread, T10Y3M), volatility (VIX monthly mean), credit & funding (Chicago Fed NFCI), energy (WTI monthly mean), the dollar (broad index, year-over-year), and breadth from Sector Watch — then maps the average through a frozen calibration: the ward's calmest month since 2007 reads 10, its November 2008 peak reads 90. Gauge anchors are provisional; they are percentile-checked against each gauge's full history in the anchor-validation report and published in the collector source.

Sector Watch fine print: the eleven-ticker panel is collected manually from a public quote feed (Yahoo's chart endpoint, an undocumented transport) whose redistribution rights are unresolved — so only derived states and percent moves publish, never prices, and scheduled collection stays disabled until a licensed source is secured. The percent figures are price returns over 22 trading sessions, not calendar-month total returns: dividends and distributions are not counted, which can make a fund look slightly weaker than its total return. State boundaries, exactly: at or above −2.0% is 🟢 steady; below −2.0% down to and including −7.0% is 🟡 softening; below −7.0% is 🔴 stressed. Monthly means for the official gauges are computed at collection time — a reading collected before month-end is a partial month until recomputed.

Breadth, fully reproducible: weakness share = (0.5 × softening + stressed) ÷ 11, expressed 0–100. That share then maps to gauge stress through these published anchors (linear between points): weakness 0 → stress 5 · 10 → 22 · 20 → 40 · 35 → 60 · 55 → 80 · 80+ → 100. Worked example from a real collection: 4 softening + 1 stressed = (0.5×4+1)÷11 = 27.3 weakness → interpolating between the 20→40 and 35→60 anchors gives stress 50. Every number on the ward card reproduces from this paragraph.
